## Cards autorais a gerar (estilo AnKing — note type AnKingOverhaul, Text + Extra)

Tags de cada um: `NEBLI::bioq-20-pentoses-radicais-livres` + `NEBLI::autoral`.

**1.1a — três destinos**
- Text: `Glucose-6-phosphate has three fates: {{c1::storage as glycogen}}, {{c1::energy via glycolysis}}, and {{c1::building via the pentose phosphate pathway}}`
- Extra: `The pentose pathway is the "building" exit — it makes no ATP.`

**1.3a — fosfato-etiqueta**
- Text: `NADPH and NADH differ only by a {{c1::phosphate}} group, which acts as a {{c1::tag}} enzymes read to keep the two electron pools from mixing`
- Extra: `Same redox chemistry, opposite jobs. A structurally trivial, functionally decisive difference.`

**1.3b — papéis opostos**
- Text: `{{c1::NADH}} carries electrons from catabolism to the respiratory chain (makes ATP); {{c1::NADPH}} donates electrons to biosynthesis and antioxidant defense`
- Extra: `One takes electrons to burn fuel, the other to build and protect.`

**1.3c — razões opostas**
- Text: `The cell keeps {{c1::NADPH/NADP+ high}} (plenty of NADPH ready to donate) and {{c1::NAD+/NADH ~1000}} (plenty of NAD+ ready to accept)`
- Extra: `Separate ratios keep the anabolic and catabolic fluxes independent.`

**2.1a — duas fases**
- Text: `The pentose phosphate pathway has an {{c1::oxidative}} phase ({{c1::irreversible}}, makes NADPH + CO2 + pentose) and a {{c1::non-oxidative}} phase ({{c1::reversible}}, rearranges sugars, no NADPH)`
- Extra: `The phases are independent, so the cell runs each at its own rate depending on what it needs.`

**2.3a — enzimas por nome**
- Text: `In the non-oxidative phase, {{c1::transketolase}} transfers {{c1::2}}-carbon units and {{c1::transaldolase}} transfers {{c1::3}}-carbon units`
- Extra: `They reshuffle 3–7C sugars without making or spending NADPH. Transketolase needs thiamine (B1).`

**2.3b — elo com glicólise**
- Text: `The non-oxidative phase connects to glycolysis because its products {{c1::F6P and G3P}} are glycolytic intermediates, and being {{c1::reversible}} it can also build ribose from them`
- Extra: `This "revolving door" lets a cell make ribose without ever running the oxidative phase.`

**2.4a — só ribose**
- Text: `When a cell needs {{c1::only ribose}} (e.g. a dividing cell), it runs {{c1::only the non-oxidative phase}}, making ribose from F6P/G3P without touching G6PD`
- Extra: `No NADPH is made in this scenario.`

**2.4b — muito NADPH / pouca ribose**
- Text: `When a cell needs {{c1::much NADPH and little ribose}} (lipogenic tissue), it runs the oxidative phase and then {{c1::recycles the excess ribose back to F6P/G3P}} to feed more NADPH`
- Extra: `Maximizes NADPH output — the classic fatty-acid-synthesizing tissue pattern.`

**2.4c — NADPH + ATP**
- Text: `When a cell needs NADPH {{c1::and ATP}}, it runs both phases and sends the resulting {{c1::G3P down glycolysis}} for energy`
- Extra: `Both branches active; the sugar skeleton ends up burned for ATP.`

**2.5a — inibição por produto**
- Text: `The rate-limiting enzyme {{c1::G6PD}} is {{c1::inhibited by NADPH}} and {{c1::activated by NADP+}} — classic product inhibition`
- Extra: `The product of the pathway shuts off the enzyme that makes it.`

**2.5b — termostato**
- Text: `The real sensor of pentose phosphate flux is the {{c1::NADPH/NADP+ ratio}}: a high ratio {{c1::slows}} the pathway, a low ratio {{c1::speeds}} it`
- Extra: `A chemical thermostat — the pathway matches its own consumption, no external hormone needed.`
